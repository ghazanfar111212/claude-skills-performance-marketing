# Packaging Playbook - Titles, Thumbnails, Hooks

Packaging is title + thumbnail + first 30 seconds. It decides whether a video is watched at all. Titles and thumbnails are judged together as a single promise; the hook then keeps the promise from breaking.

## The per-idea template

Use this compact block per idea (deviate to prose only if the user asks):

```
IDEA: <one-line concept>
Engine: Search | Browse
Format: <long-form / Short / tutorial / story / etc.> · ~<length>
Titles:
  1. <title option A>
  2. <title option B>
  3. <title option C>
Thumbnail: <subject + emotion + ≤4 words of text + contrast/visual hook>
Hook (0 - 20s): <1 - 2 lines: restate promise, raise stakes, state the payoff>
Why it works: <one honest line tied to observed demand/angle>
```

## Title patterns that pull clicks

Twist the concept through one of these - pick the pattern that fits the promise, don't force all of them:

- **Number + payoff**: "7 [things] that [outcome]." Concrete, scannable.
- **How to X without Y**: removes the objection ("How to grow on YouTube without showing your face").
- **Transformation**: "I [did X] for [time] - here's what happened."
- **Contrarian**: "Stop [common advice]," "[Popular thing] is a scam," "You're doing X wrong."
- **Curiosity gap**: "The [topic] mistake nobody talks about." Promise a secret - then actually deliver it.
- **Stakes / urgency**: "Do this before [event/deadline]," "This almost ruined my [thing]."
- **Specificity**: exact numbers, timeframes, named things beat vague claims ("$0 to $10k" > "how I made money").
- **Search-match**: for evergreen ideas, mirror the phrase people type ("how to X for beginners") - clarity beats cleverness here.

Title hygiene:
- Front-load the payoff; the strongest word shouldn't be at the end where it gets truncated.
- Keep the core promise readable at a glance (~mid-length; long titles get cut off on mobile).
- One promise per title. Two competing promises dilute the click.
- Never promise what the video can't deliver - a betrayed viewer tanks retention, which tanks reach.

## Thumbnail concepts

The thumbnail should add information the title doesn't, so together they say more than either alone.

- **Face + clear emotion** (when the niche suits it): a genuine, exaggerated expression reads instantly and humanizes.
- **Before / after** or **transformation**: split frame showing the change.
- **One big object / focal point**: uncluttered, one subject, obvious at small size.
- **Text ≤ 3 - 4 words**: not the title repeated - a second hook ("$0", "IT WORKED?", "DON'T").
- **Contrast & readability**: bold subject vs simple background; readable as a thumbnail-sized square on a phone.
- **Curiosity object**: something that raises a question the title answers.

Test: shrink it mentally to the size of a fingernail. Is the subject still obvious and the emotion/hook still legible? If not, simplify.

## Hooks (first 15 - 30 seconds)

Retention in the first 30 seconds disproportionately decides a video's reach. The hook's job: confirm they're in the right place and make leaving feel like a loss.

Effective hook moves (combine 2 - 3):
- **Restate the promise** the packaging made, immediately - no long intro, no "hey guys welcome back."
- **Raise the stakes**: why this matters, what's at risk, what they'll miss.
- **Preview the payoff**: "By the end of this you'll have X" or tease the most surprising moment.
- **Pattern interrupt**: a bold claim, a surprising visual, a fast result up front.
- **Open a loop**: pose the question the video answers; don't answer it yet.

Anti-patterns: long branded intros, "before we start, smash like," slow throat-clearing, apologizing, or burying the promise. Get to value fast.

## Packaging-first thinking

For Browse/entertainment ideas especially, it's often smarter to invent the **title + thumbnail first**, and only make the video if you can package it irresistibly. If you can't package a concept into a click, it may be a fine topic but a weak video. Package, then film.
