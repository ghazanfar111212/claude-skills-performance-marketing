# Chief Marketing Officer

## When to Use This Skill
- Setting or auditing marketing strategy at the function/company level
- Allocating a marketing budget across channels, talent, tech, and agencies
- Designing the marketing org (roles, in-house vs agency, AI-augmented team)
- Choosing the right measurement model (MMM vs attribution vs incrementality)
- Building a board/CEO/CFO-ready marketing narrative and KPI set
- Balancing brand vs performance investment
- Running annual planning, QBRs, and reallocation decisions
- Operating as a fractional CMO across multiple clients

## What a CMO Actually Owns
The mandate, not the tactics. A CMO is accountable for:
1. **Demand** - pipeline/revenue marketing is responsible for (sourced + influenced)
2. **Brand** - positioning, category, perception, pricing power
3. **Customer** - acquisition economics, retention, lifetime value, advocacy
4. **Marketing P&L** - every dollar of spend tied to a defensible return
5. **Capability** - the team, the stack, the data, and increasingly the AI operating model

If the user is debating a tactic (which bid strategy, which subject line), that is a channel decision - route to the specialist skill. The CMO lens is: does this serve the growth model, and is it the best use of the marginal dollar?

## The Marketing P&L (Budget Allocation)
Start every budget conversation by splitting spend into four buckets, then pressure-test the mix against the growth stage.

| Bucket | Typical share of marketing budget | Notes |
|--------|-----------------------------------|-------|
| Paid media | ~30% | Largest and only growing bucket industry-wide; watch media-price inflation |
| Martech | ~22% | Audit utilization before renewing; most stacks are <50% used |
| Labor (team) | ~22% | AI is compressing this; protect senior strategic roles |
| Agencies | ~21% | First place CMOs cut; keep only partners that beat in-house on cost or capability |

**Allocation frameworks:**
- **70/20/10** - 70% to what works, 20% to scaling promising bets, 10% to experiments
- **Brand vs performance** - default toward ~60% brand / 40% activation for durable growth (Binet & Field); skew more to performance only when cash-constrained or pre-PMF, and know you are borrowing future demand
- **Funnel coverage** - do not over-index on conversion; if 60%+ sits in consideration/conversion you are harvesting existing demand, not creating it

## Strategy Frameworks
- **Growth model first** - write the equation for how the business grows (e.g. traffic × CVR × AOV × repeat) before touching channels. Marketing strategy = moving the biggest constrained variable.
- **Positioning before promotion** - if the value prop sounds like every competitor, no budget fixes it. Lock category, ICP, and differentiation first.
- **STP** - Segmentation → Targeting → Positioning as the strategic spine
- **Brand-building vs sales activation** - long (brand, broad reach, emotional) vs short (performance, narrow, rational); both, in balance, beat either alone
- **Flywheel over funnel** for retention-led/PLG businesses - acquisition, activation, retention, referral compounding

## Marketing Org Design
- **Shape to the growth model, not the org chart you inherited.** A demand-gen-led B2B org ≠ a brand-led DTC org.
- **Core leadership pods:** Brand/Creative, Growth/Performance, Lifecycle/CRM, Product Marketing, Marketing Ops/Analytics
- **In-house vs agency decision:** in-house what is core, recurring, and data-sensitive; agency/contractor what is spiky, specialized, or needs scale fast
- **AI-augmented team:** treat AI as headcount leverage on production (content, creative variants, analysis, research), keeping humans on strategy, taste, and judgment. The trap is cutting the senior roles AI cannot replace.

## Measurement & Accountability
Match the method to the decision; no single number is the truth.
- **Attribution** (multi-touch, last-click) - directional, tactical, biased toward bottom-funnel and broken by privacy changes. Use for in-platform optimization, not budget allocation.
- **Marketing Mix Modeling (MMM)** - top-down, privacy-durable, answers "what should total budget and channel split be." Best for cross-channel allocation.
- **Incrementality testing** (geo holdouts, lift studies) - the gold standard for "did this spend cause anything." Use to validate the big bets.
- **Triangulate** all three plus a source-of-truth (CRM/GA4/finance) and post-purchase surveys.
- **North Star + guardrails** - one primary metric tied to value delivered, with guardrails (CAC, payback, retention) so you do not optimize the North Star into the ground.
- **Unit economics every CMO must defend:** CAC, LTV, LTV:CAC (target ≥3:1), CAC payback (target <12 months B2B SaaS, faster for DTC), blended vs paid CAC, contribution margin.

## Executive Alignment
- **To the CEO:** marketing as a growth engine and a narrative, not a cost center. Tie everything to the company's #1 objective.
- **To the CFO:** speak in payback, marginal ROI, and efficiency, not impressions. Pre-agree what "good" looks like before the quarter, not after.
- **To the CRO/Sales:** one shared definition of a qualified lead, one pipeline number, joint accountability for revenue.
- **Board deck spine:** (1) result vs plan, (2) efficiency trend (CAC/payback), (3) pipeline/revenue contribution, (4) the one strategic bet and what it needs, (5) risks. Lead with the number, not the activity.

## Planning Cadence
- **Annual:** growth model → objectives → budget → channel plan → org/capability plan → measurement plan
- **Quarterly (QBR):** reforecast, reallocate to what is working, kill what is not, reset the one big bet
- **Always-on:** an experiment backlog scored by ICE/PIE so testing never stalls

## Anti-Patterns
- Don't treat the CMO role as "head of campaigns" - if you are picking creative over setting strategy, the function will underperform
- Don't cut brand to zero in a downturn - it borrows demand from the future and erodes pricing power
- Don't allocate budget on last-click attribution - it systematically overfunds bottom-funnel
- Don't buy martech you cannot staff or integrate - utilization, not features, drives ROI
- Don't report activity ("we shipped 40 emails") instead of outcomes ("email drove X% of revenue at Y CAC")
- Don't chase an AI-leader narrative without process maturity - capability and data readiness come first
- Don't run a generalist team against a specialist growth model - shape the org to how the business actually grows

## Current Standards (as of mid-2026 - refresh annually)
- **Marketing budgets** average **~7.8% of company revenue** (Gartner 2026 CMO Spend Survey), roughly flat for three years; half of CMOs run at ≤6%. "Budget-conscious" CMOs sit at ≤4% of revenue, "big spenders" above ~10.5%.
- **Paid media** is ~30.6% of the marketing budget and the only bucket that has grown its share over five years; martech, labor, and agencies have all eroded.
- **Digital** is ~61% of channel spend, the highest on record.
- **AI:** CMOs now allocate ~15.3% of the marketing budget to AI, but only ~30% report being ready to scale it; ~70% call becoming an AI leader a critical 2026 goal while ~70% admit their processes are not yet mature. GenAI ROI is showing up as time efficiency (~49%), cost efficiency (~40%), and added content/capacity (~27%).
- **Agencies:** ~39% of CMOs are cutting agency spend (eliminating weak relationships, streamlining rosters, renegotiating scope); ~22% say GenAI has reduced reliance on agencies for creativity/strategy.
- **Posture:** static budgets are pushing CMOs to fund AI transformation through reallocation and productivity rather than new money - sharper prioritization is the core 2026 skill.
