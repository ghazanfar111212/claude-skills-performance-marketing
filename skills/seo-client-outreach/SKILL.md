# SEO Client Outreach (LinkedIn + Free Audit)

## When to Use This Skill
- Finding and qualifying SEO prospects on LinkedIn across mixed verticals
- Turning a free SEO audit into the central outreach hook (not a throwaway "free audit" line)
- Optimizing the sender's LinkedIn profile so prospects say yes after they click it
- Writing connection requests and DM sequences specifically for SEO offers
- Producing a fast pre-sale audit without prospect account access
- Designing the SEO offer/pricing for local service, ecommerce, and B2B/SaaS clients
- Booking the audit-walkthrough call and converting it to a retainer

**Sister skills - use the right lane:** email infra/sequences → `cold-email-outreach`; niche & positioning decisions → `agency-positioning-expert`; building the proof assets → `case-study-writer`; the cross-sell after the SEO foot-in-door → `google-ads-expert` / `landing-page-designer`; automating audit production at scale → `n8n-workflow-expert`.

## The Core Mechanism (read this first)

The whole play is one loop, and every section below serves it:

> **Targeted prospect → connection → a *specific finding already spotted* → offer the full free audit → audit-walkthrough call → retainer.**

The free audit is NOT the opener. A naked "want a free SEO audit?" is the most ignored message on LinkedIn - every agency sends it. The audit is the *payoff*. The opener is **one specific, true thing you already found about their site.** Proof you looked beats any offer.

## Step 1: Targeting (Sales Navigator)

Mixed verticals means you target by *buyer*, not industry alone. The person who buys SEO is rarely "the CEO" except at small local businesses.

| Vertical | Who buys SEO | Sales Nav filters | Buying signal that they need you |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Local service** (dental, home services, legal, med-spa) | Owner / Practice Manager / Office Manager | Geography + small headcount (1 - 50) + title | Not in the local map pack for their money keyword; thin or no website |
| **Ecommerce** | Founder / Head of Ecommerce / Marketing Manager | "Shopify/Magento" in profile, DTC keywords, 11 - 200 size | Strong paid spend, weak organic; thin category pages |
| **B2B / SaaS** | Head of Demand Gen / Content / Growth / VP Marketing | SaaS industry + Marketing function + 11 - 500 size | Competitors own the category/comparison terms; low blog traffic |

**Targeting rules**
- One vertical per campaign batch. Mixed *pipeline*, focused *batches* - the audit angle and DM copy change per vertical, so don't blend them in a single send list.
- Use Sales Nav **lead filters** (people) not just account filters. Save leads to a named list per batch.
- Prioritize accounts already spending on paid (they value traffic and have budget) - visible via ad libraries or "we're hiring a PPC manager" posts.
- Avoid companies with an in-house SEO team or an agency logo in recent posts - wrong timing.

## Step 2: Profile-as-Landing-Page (do this BEFORE you send anything)

On LinkedIn the prospect checks *your* profile before replying. A weak profile kills the best DM. Fix these five, in order:

1. **Headline** - outcome, not job title. ❌ "Digital Marketer | SEO Expert" ✅ "I help [vertical] rank on Google without paying for every click - SEO that compounds."
2. **Banner** - one proof point + who you help (a result, a logo strip, or a single before/after metric).
3. **Featured section** - pin ONE thing: a sample audit (Loom or PDF) or a case study. This is what they click after your DM. (Build the case study with `case-study-writer`.)
4. **About** - first two lines visible before "see more" must state the ICP + the result. Then proof, then a soft CTA.
5. **Activity** - post 2 - 3x/week on SEO wins/teardowns so the profile looks alive. A dead profile reads as a bot.

> Offshore credibility note: a polished profile, a real face photo, specific US-client results, and consistent posting do more to defuse the "Pakistan-based?" objection than anything you can say in a DM. Let the profile carry it.

## Step 3: The Connection Request

- **Send during US business hours**, not Pakistan night-batch all at once. Schedule around the prospect's timezone.
- Test **no-note vs. short-note** - no-note often has higher *accept* rates, but a sharp 1-liner pre-qualifies and warms the later DM. For SEO, lead with the note.
- The note is NOT a pitch. It's a reason you're connecting that references *them*.

**Good notes**
- "Saw {{company}} ranks for [term] but [competitor] is outranking you on [related term] - connecting, I geek out on this stuff."
- "Your post on [topic] - we just helped a [vertical] client fix the exact thing you mentioned. Connecting."

**Bad notes (never)**
- "I'd love to offer you a free SEO audit!"
- "I help businesses grow with SEO, let's connect."
- Anything with a Calendly link in the connection request.

## Step 4: The DM Sequence (after they accept)

Do NOT pitch the instant they accept. Sequence over ~12 days, each message standalone-valuable. No subject lines (this is DM, not email - that's the `cold-email-outreach` lane).

- **Day 1 - 2 - the finding.** Open with the specific thing you spotted. End with a soft question, not an offer.
  > "Thanks for connecting. Quick one - I noticed {{company}} doesn't have a page targeting '[money keyword]', but [competitor] gets ~[X] visits/mo from it. Is organic something you're actively working on right now?"
- **Day 3 - 4 - the audit offer (only if they engaged, or as a value drop if not).** Offer the full audit as a 3 - 5 min Loom. Low friction, no call ask yet.
  > "I went ahead and recorded a 4-min teardown of your site - 3 quick wins + the gap vs [competitor]. Want me to send it over? No strings."
- **Day 7 - the bump.** "Did the teardown make sense? Happy to walk through the 2 - 3 highest-impact fixes live if useful."
- **Day 12 - soft breakup.** "I'll stop cluttering your inbox - want me to just drop the audit doc so you have it regardless?" (The breakup pulls strong replies; the doc-drop gives a graceful yes.)

**Sequence principles**
- Vary length: short finding → medium offer → short bump → short breakup.
- Reply within the same thread.
- If they reply positively at any step, drop the sequence and move to the audit/call immediately - speed wins.

## Step 5: Producing the Audit (fast, no account access)

You're auditing pre-sale, so you usually have **no** Search Console / GA access. Build the audit from external tools only. Target 30 - 45 min per audit; 10 min for the teaser finding.

**Toolkit (mostly free / cheap)**
- **Ahrefs or Semrush** - organic keywords, traffic estimate, top pages, backlink/DR gap vs a named competitor. The single most persuasive slide.
- **PageSpeed Insights** - Core Web Vitals (instant, visual, undeniable).
- **Screaming Frog (free, 500 URLs)** - missing titles/meta, broken links, thin/duplicate pages, indexability.
- **Manual SERP check** - search their money keywords from an incognito/US-located browser; screenshot where they rank vs competitors.
- **Local only:** search "[service] [city]" → screenshot the map pack; audit their Google Business Profile (categories, reviews, posts, photos, NAP consistency).

**What the audit must contain (and nothing more):**
1. One headline gap framed as lost revenue/traffic ("[competitor] gets ~X organic visits/mo you're not getting").
2. 2 - 3 quick wins they could action this week (builds trust, shows you're not hoarding).
3. One bigger structural issue that *needs help* (this is the wedge for the retainer).
4. A clear "here's what fixing this looks like" → the call.

**Format:** Loom (3 - 5 min) >> PDF on LinkedIn. Video builds rapport and is harder to ignore; you can still attach a 1-page PDF summary. **Never** send a 20-page generic audit - it reads as automated and nobody opens it.

**Scale it (his stack):** export tool data → feed to Claude API → auto-draft the audit narrative + quick-wins per prospect, then record the Loom over the generated outline. Orchestrate the export→draft→notify pipeline in n8n. This is what lets a lean/freelancer model run audit-led outreach at volume without it feeling templated. (See `n8n-workflow-expert`.)

## Step 6: Vertical-Specific Audit Angles

Match the finding and the audit emphasis to the vertical, or the pitch falls flat:

- **Local service:** map-pack visibility, GBP optimization, review velocity, location/service pages, local citations. Wedge: "You're invisible in the map pack for your highest-intent searches."
- **Ecommerce:** indexation & crawl waste (faceted nav), thin/duplicate category pages, missing buying-guide content, product schema, competitor blog traffic. Wedge: "You spend on ads for traffic you could own for free."
- **B2B / SaaS:** topical authority, bottom-funnel comparison/alternative pages, DR/backlink gap, content decay. Wedge: "Competitors own '[category] software' and the comparison terms - that's pipeline walking past you."

## Step 7: Offer & Pricing (US prospects)

Lead with the audit, not price. Price only after the walkthrough call. Rough US ranges:

| Vertical | Typical monthly retainer | Foot-in-door |
|---|---|---|
| Local service | $750 - $2,500/mo | Paid GBP + on-page sprint, or 1st month discounted |
| Ecommerce | $1,500 - $5,000/mo | Technical audit + category-page fix project ($500 - $1,500) |
| B2B / SaaS | $2,500 - $8,000/mo | Content + comparison-page sprint |

- **Productized audit upsell:** if they won't commit to retainer, sell the full audit + roadmap as a paid one-off ($300 - $1,000). Converts skeptics and self-funds the pipeline.
- **Avoid pure performance/rank-based pricing** with cold prospects - too much trust required and ranking timelines aren't in your control. Retainer + clear deliverables.
- Use `agency-positioning-expert` to lock the offer wording; this skill just sets the ranges and the foot-in-door logic.

## LinkedIn Account Safety (don't get restricted)

- **Connection requests:** ~15 - 25/day on a warmed account; LinkedIn throttles around **~100 - 200 invites/week** and tightens with low accept rates. Withdraw stale pending invites monthly.
- **Warm a new/dormant account** for 1 - 2 weeks (engage, post, manual connects) before any volume.
- **Automation:** technically against LinkedIn ToS and the fastest way to a restriction. If used, prefer **cloud-based** tools (HeyReach, Expandi, Dripify) over browser extensions, stay well under human-like limits, and never run it on a fresh account.
- Keep **accept rate >30%** and **reply/positive rate healthy** - bad targeting + spammy notes tank both and flag the account.
- Sales Navigator gives more search + InMail headroom and is worth it for serious volume.

## Benchmarks (rough, audit-led LinkedIn)
- Connection accept rate: 30 - 40%+ (sharp, relevant note)
- Accept → reply: 15 - 25% (with a specific finding, not a generic offer)
- Reply → audit delivered: 40 - 60%
- Audit → booked call: 20 - 35%
- The finding-first opener is the single biggest lever on reply rate - generic offers sit near 2 - 5%.

## Anti-Patterns
- Leading with "free SEO audit" instead of a specific finding - instant ignore.
- Pitching in the connection request.
- A Calendly link before any value or rapport.
- Sending a 20-page templated PDF nobody reads.
- Blasting Pakistan-night batches all at once into US inboxes/feeds.
- Running automation at full speed on a cold account (restriction risk).
- Mixing verticals in one send list (the angle breaks).
- Talking price before the walkthrough call.
- A polished DM pointing back to a weak/empty profile.
- Hoarding all the fixes - give 2 - 3 free; the wedge is the structural work.

## Compliance
- **LinkedIn ToS:** manual outreach is fine; automation/scraping is against ToS - use conservatively and at your own account risk.
- **US:** no email-style CAN-SPAM concern for LinkedIn DMs, but respect opt-outs immediately - "remove me" = stop, permanently.
- **Data:** don't scrape and store prospect data at scale from a fresh account; build the list through Sales Nav properly.
