# Gap Detection - Signals, Search Recipes & Traps

## Search recipes (the instrument)

Run these with web_search, adapting `<topic>` and `<audience>`:

**Demand discovery**
- `<topic> youtube` - see what ranks and how it's packaged.
- `how to <topic>`, `<topic> for beginners`, `best <topic>`, `<topic> vs`, `<topic> not working`, `<topic> 2026` - walk the autocomplete/question landscape.
- `site:reddit.com <topic>` - repeated questions, "is there a good video on X," bad-answer threads.
- `<topic> tiktok` / `<topic> shorts` - short-platform heat that hasn't reached long-form.

**Supply assessment**
- Sample the top YouTube results for the head term and 3 - 5 long-tails. For each, note: how old (date in title/upload), how big the channel, how thin/deep, and how good the packaging (titles/thumbnails) is.
- A results page full of 2-3-year-old videos, tiny channels, or weak titles = an opening. A page of fresh, deep, well-packaged videos from strong channels = saturated.

**Outlier / proven-concept hunting**
- Look for a video with views many multiples of its channel's subscriber count, or one hit sitting in an otherwise thin topic. Capture the angle, promise, format, and timing that made it work.

## Reading the signals

| What you observe | What it means |
|---|---|
| Many searches/questions, few or tiny videos | Demand - supply gap - strong opening |
| Top videos all 2+ years old on a topic that changed | Freshness gap - win on current version |
| Top videos thin / bad audio / don't answer the question | Quality gap - win by being genuinely better |
| Topic only in blogs, or only in long streams / only in Shorts | Format gap - serve the missing format |
| Every video takes the same angle | Angle gap - contrarian or method-specific take is open |
| Covered generically, comments ask "for X?" | Audience gap - segment-target it (easiest win) |
| Good topic, uniformly weak titles/thumbnails | Packaging gap - out-package the incumbents |
| Small channel got a huge hit here | Validated demand - adapt the concept |
| High demand + fresh, deep, well-packaged incumbents | TRAP - likely saturated, flag it |

## Comment mining (highest-signal, most overlooked)

On the popular videos in a niche, read the top comments and replies for:
- Explicit requests: "can you do one for…", "what about…", "please make a video on…". A highly-liked request is served demand nobody has answered.
- Failure reports: "this didn't work because I have X" - a whole video hides in that objection.
- Follow-up questions the video didn't answer - the sequel or the "part 2 for [segment]" gap.

Weight requests by likes/replies; a request echoed by many people is a near-guaranteed hit.

## Channel-fit reality check

A gap is only *your* gap if the channel can win it. Before ranking a gap high, ask:
- Can this channel be **credible** on it? (Audience trust, on-brand.)
- Can it be produced with what they **have**? (No fantasy budgets.)
- For head terms owned by giants: can the user win via **freshness, a sharper angle, a neglected sub-audience, or better packaging** rather than out-resourcing? If not, downgrade or flag as a trap.

Smaller channels win angle, audience, freshness, and packaging gaps - not raw head-term battles. Bias recommendations accordingly.

## From gap to video

Every gap should hand off cleanly to youtube-video-ideation: the gap type dictates the winning move (freshness → make the current version; audience → target the segment in the title; packaging → invest in title/thumbnail), and the "Make this" line becomes the seed the ideation skill packages into titles, thumbnail, and hook.
