# Message Frameworks - LinkedIn Outreach Copy

Copy patterns for every motion and every step. Adapt the bracketed parts to the ICP/offer. Keep the copy rules from SKILL.md: one ask, vary length, reference *them*, no links in connection notes, fallbacks on every variable, no fake results.

## The four-beat arc (all motions)

1. **Hook** - the reason you reached out + a soft question. No pitch, no link.
2. **Payoff** - offer the useful thing; strip friction; no call yet.
3. **Bump** (4-touch only) - nudge + offer to walk through the top fixes.
4. **Breakup** - back off, plant the real next step, offer the payoff as an easy yes.

## The 7 opener motions (Hook = Msg 1)

**1. Finding-led** - you spotted something specific about *them*. Highest reply; needs a per-lead check (automate with Clay/n8n).
> "Thanks for connecting, {{firstName}}. Quick one - I checked [specific thing] for {{companyName}} and [specific true finding: they're missing X / a competitor beats them on Y]. Most [role]s have no idea this is happening. Is [the outcome] something you're working on right now?"

**2. Insight/POV-led** - a real pattern you've observed in their space. Scalable when per-lead findings aren't feasible.
> "Thanks for connecting, {{firstName}}. Quick one from what I do - I work on [discipline], and [their space] is one of the [worst-covered / biggest-gap] areas I see: [the pattern, stated as observed fact]. As [why-now], it's a quiet leak. Has [the outcome] been on your radar for {{companyName}}?"

**3. Trigger-led** - reference a real event.
> "Congrats on [funding / new role / launch], {{firstName}}. Usually when [that event] happens, [related need] jumps up the list - is [outcome] something the team's looking at right now?"

**4. Content-led** - engage with their post first, then DM.
> "Your post on [topic] stuck with me - especially [specific point]. We just [did the relevant thing] for a [their-space] company. Curious how you're thinking about [related angle]?"

**5. Question/research-led** - a genuine expert question (consultative/high-ticket).
> "{{firstName}}, a real question since you run [function] at {{companyName}} - how are you currently handling [specific problem]? Asking because I keep seeing [pattern] and I'm trying to sanity-check it against how good teams do it."

**6. Give-led** - lead with a genuinely useful asset.
> "{{firstName}}, made a [benchmark / checklist / teardown] for [their role] on [topic] - [one concrete thing it reveals]. Want it? No ask, just useful."

**7. Referral/mutual-led** - mutual connection or group.
> "{{firstName}}, we're both [in / connected through] [group/person] - and you're exactly the kind of [role] I wanted to reach on [topic]. [One-line reason]. Worth a quick exchange?"

## Payoff (Msg 2) - offer the useful thing, no call yet

Keep it short, specific to them, and explicitly low-friction.
> "No worries if it's not front of mind, {{firstName}}. I actually ran the check for {{companyName}} - [what you have: screenshot/teardown/gap analysis], plus a couple of things you could tweak. Want me to send it? Quick skim, no call."

## Bump (Msg 3, 4-touch only)

> "Did that [teardown/breakdown] make sense, {{firstName}}? Happy to walk through the 2 - 3 fastest fixes if it's useful."

## Breakup (final)

> "I'll stop popping up, {{firstName}}. If it's ever useful, I can show you why some [their-space] [companies] [win the outcome] and others don't - usually one or two structural things, ~15 min. Either way, want me to drop the [payoff] so you've got it? Good luck with {{companyName}}."

The breakup does two jobs: it reliably pulls replies (loss aversion), and the payoff-drop gives a graceful low-commitment yes.

## The manual "yes-send-it" reply (the most important message)

Not automated. Sent by hand when they reply yes. Structure:
> "Here you go, {{firstName}} - this is [proof, e.g. ChatGPT answering '[their query]']:
> [the screenshot/asset - them missing, competitor named]
>
> Two things you can do yourself right now: [quick win 1] and [quick win 2] - those help at the margins.
> But the real reason [outcome happens for competitors and not them] is [structural gap]. That's the piece that actually [moves the needle], and it's not a 5-minute fix.
> Happy to walk you through what fixing it looks like - 15 min this week?"

Rule: **give the 2 free wins (trust), wedge on the structural gap (the call).** Free wins without the wedge → "thanks" and gone. Wedge without free wins → feels like a pitch. You need both.

## Honest authority (no fake track record)

- Claim **discipline expertise** ("I do this for a living") + **an observed pattern** in their space - both true.
- Never invent case studies/numbers. A sharp buyer detects it and it's unrecoverable.
- The instant a real result exists, swap it into the hook - "I just helped a [their-space] company go from [before] to [after]" outperforms any pattern claim.

## Tone calibration

- **Matter-of-fact practitioner** (default): confident, plain, no hype.
- **Direct**: shorter, more assertive; good for senior/busy buyers.
- **Soft/curious**: gentler question framing; good for relationship-heavy or sensitive spaces.
  Keep one tone consistent across the whole sequence - don't mix.

## Personalization tiers (match to motion + automation)

- **Per-lead finding** - highest reply (~15 - 25%). Requires a real per-lead check; scale with Clay/n8n + a Claude column drafting the finding.
- **Category insight** - scalable (~8 - 12%). Same copy across the vertical; the insight is true at the segment level.
- **Generic** - ~2 - 5%; avoid. If a message could be sent to anyone, rewrite it.

## Length & format

- Connection note: under ~300 chars, one idea, references them, no link.
- Msg 1: 2 - 4 sentences. Msg 2: 2 - 4. Bump/breakup: 1 - 3.
- Mobile-readable: short lines, no walls of text, one ask.
