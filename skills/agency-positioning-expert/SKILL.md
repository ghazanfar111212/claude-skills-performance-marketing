# Agency Positioning Expert

## When to Use This Skill
- Deciding whether to niche down (and into what)
- Evaluating multiple vertical options
- Designing or redesigning agency offers
- Defining/redefining ICP
- Writing positioning statements for website, LinkedIn, cold outreach
- Pricing strategy decisions
- Deciding between generalist vs specialist positioning

## The Core Positioning Formula

```
[Agency] helps [specific ICP]
achieve [specific outcome]
through [specific mechanism]
unlike [category alternatives]
```

**Example:**
> We help dental implant practices in US metros generate 15-30 high-ticket consultations per month through a proven Google Ads + landing page + follow-up system - unlike generic marketing agencies that treat dental the same as plumbing.

**Test**: If you can swap "dental implant practices" for "any service business" and the statement still works, your positioning isn't specific enough.

## Niche Selection Framework

### The 4-Lens Evaluation

Score each potential vertical on:

**1. Pain Lens (1-5): How much does the pain hurt?**
- High: They lose sleep over the problem
- Low: Nice-to-have, not critical

**2. Purchasing Power Lens (1-5): Can they afford you?**
- High: $3K+/month retainers are normal
- Low: Price sensitive, every dollar scrutinized

**3. Reachability Lens (1-5): Can you find them cost-effectively?**
- High: Concentrated on specific platforms, searchable lists
- Low: Diffuse, hard to identify/target

**4. Proof Lens (1-5): Do you have credibility?**
- High: Case studies, connections, lived experience
- Low: Zero prior work in this vertical

Score each candidate vertical. Top scorers in pain + purchasing power + reachability are usually winners. Proof can be built.

Based on the framework above, these consistently score well:

### The "Wedge Vertical" Strategy
If you can't decide on one niche:
1. Pick a **beachhead** (one specific vertical where you have strongest proof)
2. Use proof there to expand to **adjacent verticals** later
3. Don't call yourself "the [vertical] agency" - position as "I specialize in [beachhead] and [related verticals]"

Example: Start with dental implants → expand to medspa/aesthetics (similar ICP: service business, high ticket, local) → then to cosmetic surgery.

## ICP Definition

### Beyond Demographics

**Surface ICP**: Dental practices in the US
**Real ICP**: Single-location dental practices in US metros with $1-3M revenue, 10-25 years operating, owner-operated (not corporate DSO), 3+ dentists, actively running Google Ads (shows marketing willingness), and have at least one specialty service (implants, orthodontics, cosmetic).

### The ICP Triangulation Questions
1. **Who pays you fastest?** (healthy cash flow client)
2. **Who refers you consistently?** (advocacy client)
3. **Who gets best results?** (case study client)
4. **Who's hardest to service?** (anti-ICP)

Overlap of 1-3 = your true ICP.
#4 = your anti-ICP (document and disqualify).

### Disqualification Criteria (Anti-ICP)
Explicitly name who you don't work with:
- Revenue below X
- Tried 3+ agencies already (probably the problem)
- Unrealistic timeline expectations
- Won't commit to minimum engagement length
- Can't provide access to platforms/data
- Haggling on pricing before value conversation

## Offer Design

### Offer Stack Structure

**Tier 1: Entry offer (reduce risk, prove value)**
- 30-day audit + strategy + first-month execution
- Fixed-price: $2-5K
- Purpose: Trust-building, not profit

**Tier 2: Core retainer (your bread & butter)**
- Monthly ongoing management
- $3-8K/month typical for paid media
- 3-6 month minimum commitment
- Includes: ads management, reporting, strategy calls, creative input

**Tier 3: Scale/growth package (for top-tier clients)**
- Full-funnel: paid media + landing pages + CRM + creative
- $8-20K/month
- Quarterly strategic reviews
- Dedicated account team

### Offer Design Principles
- **Specific outcome, not process**: "20 qualified dental consultations/month" not "Google Ads management"
- **Guarantee or risk reversal**: Pilot period, performance clauses, money-back
- **Clear deliverables**: What they get each month, explicitly listed
- **Included vs. add-on**: Prevent scope creep with clear boundaries
- **Onboarding process**: Named steps, timeline, milestones

### Productized vs. Custom
- **Productized**: Fixed scope, fixed price, faster sales, lower profit per client
- **Custom**: Scoped per client, variable price, slower sales, higher profit per client

Most successful agencies productize entry offer, customize retainer.

## Pricing Strategy

### Pricing Models

**1. % of Ad Spend**
- Traditional model: 10-20% of spend
- Pro: Scales with client success
- Con: Incentive misalignment (bigger spend ≠ better results)

**2. Flat Retainer**
- Fixed monthly fee regardless of spend
- Pro: Predictable revenue, clean margins
- Con: Caps your upside on big accounts

**3. Performance-Based**
- Pay per lead, per sale, or % of revenue
- Pro: Aligned incentives, easier sell
- Con: Payment timing risk, attribution disputes

**4. Hybrid (Most Agencies)**
- Base retainer + performance bonus
- Example: $3K/month + $50/qualified lead above baseline
- Best of both worlds if structured right

### Pricing Principles
- **Anchor high, offer tiers**: Show the $15K package to justify the $5K one
- **Never discount - reduce scope**: Protect your pricing integrity
- **Minimum engagements**: 3-6 months protects from tire-kickers
- **Annual contracts at discount**: Reduces churn, improves cash flow
- **Raise prices annually**: For existing clients, with clear value justification

### Pricing Red Flags
- Prospect asks for pricing on first call (before discovery)
- "What's the cheapest you can do?"
- "Our last agency did it for $X" (race to bottom)
- Wanting month-to-month only

## Positioning Assets

### Where Positioning Shows Up

**1. Website Homepage (above fold)**
- Headline = core positioning statement
- Subhead = specific ICP + outcome
- Hero image = ICP-recognizable (don't use generic stock)

**2. LinkedIn Headline**
- Not "CEO at [Agency]" - leave that for title line
- Use format: "I help [ICP] [outcome] through [mechanism]"

**3. Cold Email Signature**
- 1-line positioning under your name
- One proof point (case study snippet)

**4. Pitch Deck Slide 2**
- "What we do" slide
- ICP + outcome + mechanism in a single sentence

**5. Podcast/Content Intros**
- Your one-liner should be memorable and specific
- Test it: does the listener remember what you do?

## Generalist vs Specialist

### When Generalist Works
- High referral flow from existing network
- Major account controls 50%+ of revenue
- You're 1-2 years old, still exploring

### When Specialist Wins
- Cold outbound is primary acquisition
- Competing against other agencies for work
- Want premium pricing power
- Building media/content presence

### The Hybrid Path (Most Mature Agencies)
- Public positioning: Specialist (for marketing leverage)
- Actual client mix: Some vertical expansion for retention/revenue
- Never explicitly advertise "we do anything"

## Competitive Differentiation

### Differentiation Types (Rank by Strength)

**1. Proprietary Method (Strongest)**
- Named framework you own and can't be copied easily
- Example: "The STAG Method for Google Ads" - name + steps
- Requires real substance underneath

**2. Vertical Expertise**
- Deep knowledge, case studies, network in one space
- Build through 10+ clients in same vertical

**3. Speed/Delivery Model**
- Faster than competitors, measurable SLAs
- Risky if you can't consistently deliver

**4. Team Composition**
- Senior-only teams, no offshore, specific certifications
- Requires premium pricing to sustain

**5. Technology/Tooling**
- Proprietary tools, dashboards, AI tools
- Defensible only if tools are genuinely better
- Aligns with AI agency positioning

### Weak Differentiation (Avoid)
- "We really care about our clients" (everyone says this)
- "Customized approach" (everyone says this)
- "Full-service agency" (negative signal in 2024+)
- Listing platforms (Google, Meta, TikTok) - not differentiation

## Decision Framework (Example: Multi-Market Paid Media Specialist)

Based on your background - multi-market paid media, AI automation, vertical case studies in dental/contractors/NDT - your strongest positioning angles:

### Option A: Vertical + Service Specialist
"I help [dental practices in North America] generate [15-30 qualified implant consultations/month] through [Google Ads + landing page system built on GHL]."
- **Pros**: Leverage existing case studies, clear positioning, higher close rate
- **Cons**: Narrow TAM initially

### Option B: Method-Led Positioning
"I help service businesses scale with the STAG Method for Google Ads - a campaign architecture built on 5 years of multi-market agency data."
- **Pros**: Own the framework, applies across verticals, IP moat
- **Cons**: Requires content/media presence to amplify

### Option C: AI-Native Agency
"I help service businesses cut CAC by 30%+ using AI-powered campaign management and automation systems."
- **Pros**: Timely, premium pricing, fewer direct competitors
- **Cons**: Have to walk the talk on AI delivery, steeper positioning work

### Option D: Hybrid (Recommended for Your Stage)
Public positioning: Dental + high-ticket service (Option A)
Internal systems branded as STAG + AI (Option B/C)
Expansion path: Dental → medspa → cosmetic surgery → legal (similar ICPs)

## Anti-Patterns
- Claiming to serve every vertical on your website
- Changing positioning every 3 months
- Copying a competitor's positioning word-for-word
- Positioning based on what you want to do vs. where you have proof
- "We do X, Y, and Z" - three services listed = no positioning
- Writing positioning for other agencies to admire, not prospects to buy
- Over-engineered frameworks nobody remembers
- Positioning without proof - just claims
- Adding disclaimers that dilute the message ("we also do...", "but we can...")
